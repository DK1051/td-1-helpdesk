Repo: td-1-helpdesk · Environment: td-1-helpdesk (Keycloak setup script) · Day: D0 · Time box: 60 minutes · Credit cap: $5

## Goal
Build the skeleton of the 1E Front Desk Casebook so Eojin can do the human work on it over the next days: a caller-verification procedure (SOP — a written step-by-step procedure) based on public guidance, a ticket template, ten realistic Maple Harbour helpdesk tickets (six ordinary break-fix tickets and four phone reset requests that are social engineering, written as caller transcripts), and knowledge-base article templates in English and Korean. 4. `builder`: create `1e-front-desk-casebook/` with:
   - `README.md` — what the casebook is (plain English), the folder map, "Eojin's part" (below) and a Status line `In progress (T1-S01)`.
   - `SOP-caller-verification.md` — an outline, not a finished SOP: Purpose; Scope (which requests this applies to: password reset, MFA reset, new device enrolment, phone/email change); Verification steps in order — (1) call back the phone number already on file, never a number the caller gives; (2) one-time code to the registered device; (3) manager approval for privileged accounts; (4) temporary password that expires in 60 minutes; (5) watch for MFA changes in the 24 hours after a reset; What to do when a step fails (block, escalate, log); Red flags seen in public reporting (urgency, a "new phone", asking to skip the callback, name-dropping a manager); What to log. Sources section quoting exactly: AL26-010 — attackers "convince support staff to reset MFA or enroll attacker-controlled devices"; "out-of-band verification procedures for identity-related requests"; "restrict and closely monitor MFA reset, recovery, and device re-enrolment processes" with "enhanced verification and approval" (URL in README); UK NCSC, "Incidents impacting retailers" (4 May 2025) — "review helpdesk password reset processes, including how the helpdesk authenticates staff members credentials" (https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/blog-post/incidents-impacting-retailers); Okta, "Cross-Tenant Impersonation: Prevention and Detection" (31 Aug 2023) — "Strengthen help desk identity verification processes using visual verification." (https://sec.okta.com/articles/2023/08/cross-tenant-impersonation-prevention-and-detection). Leave `<!-- Eojin: -->` markers where his decisions go.
   - `ticket-template.md` — fields: ID, date, requester (name, department, phone on file, email), channel (phone / email / walk-up), category, description, troubleshooting steps or call transcript, **Decision** (empty), **Reply to user** (empty), time spent, lesson learned.
   - `tickets/T01.md` … `tickets/T10.md` using the template. T01–T06: A+ break-fix across hardware, operating system and software (for example a laptop that will not power on at a dock, a Windows update loop, a printer driver, an Outlook profile, a VPN client that will not connect, a phone that cannot enrol in the company app) — each with realistic symptoms and a short troubleshooting history, Decision and Reply left empty for Eojin. T07–T10: phone reset requests that are social engineering, written as caller transcripts (caller lines and agent lines), each a different pressure: urgency, a claimed new phone, asking to skip the callback, impersonating a named manager or the IT vendor. Only behaviour described in public reporting; no technique beyond what AL26-010 describes: "convince support staff to reset MFA or enroll attacker-controlled devices".
   - `kb/en/TEMPLATE.md` and `kb/ko/TEMPLATE.md` — knowledge-base article templates (title, applies to, symptoms, steps, when to escalate, last reviewed); the Korean template written in Korean.
5. `chores`: consistency pass — every person, phone (`555-01xx`) and email (`@mapleharbour.example`) is fictional; ticket IDs T01–T10 unique; the same five or six staff names reused across tickets; no real company names except in the SOP's Sources section.
